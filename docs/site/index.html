SwarmSync: Timely evidence under unreliable connectivity

SwarmSync studies how devices exchange disaster-response observations when connectivity is unreliable and some reports cannot be trusted.

Sambhav Surana · Updated 1 November 2026
● Research in progress · Simulation-based study

Read the research plan ↗Run the local demo ↗

Abstract

Disaster-response observations may be delayed, lost, duplicated or contradicted as devices exchange reports over unreliable connections. SwarmSync is a simulation-based research project investigating how communication and evidence-validation policies affect correct, timely delivery. The planned study compares local-only, direct, full and selective sharing with fixed synthetic observations and network traces. An independent evaluator alone sees ground truth; provenance distinguishes relayed copies from independent evidence. The deterministic core, report generator and standalone evaluator have bounded test coverage, while their end-to-end integration, final policy comparisons and manuscript remain incomplete. No final comparative findings are available.

1 / Study

Six devices.
One delivery problem.

Compare local-only, direct, full and selective sharing against the same generated observations and network traces. Measure correct on-time delivery, false reports, missed results, latency and communication cost.

Only the independent evaluator sees ground truth. Reports retain their original provenance through relays: repeated copies are not independent corroboration.

2 / Comparisons

What should a device share?

01

Local only

Keep observations on the originating device. Establish a diagnostic baseline without networking.

02

Direct

Send observations straight to their required destination without peer replication.

03

Full

Share with peers, accounting for duplicates, queues and delivery costs.

04

Selective

Prioritize relevant, timely evidence. The distinct policy remains under development.

Evidence limits

Sensor observations, connectivity and compromised-device behavior are simulated assumptions. Results cannot establish real sensing accuracy, hardware security, privacy guarantees or field rescue performance. Real Wi-Fi sensing, hardware measurements and operational deployment are outside the initial study.
